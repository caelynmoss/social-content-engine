# Editorial judgment — what makes a post worth reading

A writing skill is not intelligent because its language is polished. It is intelligent when it selects an *original, defensible point* and makes the reasoning legible without making up facts.

## Turn a note into a real thesis

Privately identify:
- **Claim:** The actual assertion or question in the author's words.
- **Common assumption:** What existing intuition does it challenge or refine? Avoid straw men; when uncertain call it a possible assumption, not what "everyone thinks".
- **Mechanism:** What causal or structural explanation would make the claim true?
- **Consequence:** What changes for a real reader (builder, trader, creator, operator, ordinary user)?
- **Boundary/counterexample:** Where does the claim stop working? What credible objection deserves an answer?

Use only supported elements. If the input is merely a feeling, a clear observation may be better than a bold thesis.

## Five creative moves (choose one that fits the author)

1. **A distinction:** Two things the timeline confuses (identity vs authority; tool vs issuer; speed vs accountability).
2. **An unexpected mechanism:** A reason a familiar result occurs (incentives, control surfaces, distribution, liquidity, risk).
3. **A counterintuitive tradeoff:** A benefit with a cost; don't invent a false dilemma.
4. **A grounded micro-example:** A specific action or scenario *clearly labelled hypothetical* unless the user actually experienced it.
5. **An implication:** If this premise holds, what should someone change in how they build or evaluate something?

Do not force a novelty claim, false conflict, dramatic hook, precise number or personal story. If the author has no evidence for a controversial claim, weaken it or mark a question.

## Editing rubric (internal, not user-visible by default)

Evaluate four dimensions as pass/needs-work; don't fabricate numerical scores:
- **Insight:** Can the reader restate a specific takeaway, rather than "AI is changing everything"?
- **Reasoning:** Does at least one concrete premise justify the takeaway? Are important exceptions acknowledged when material?
- **Authenticity:** Are stance, certainty and syntax compatible with the chosen author?
- **Economy:** Can any sentence be deleted without losing meaning? Are thread segments genuinely different?

Fix the weakest dimension before returning a draft. A short sincere post beats a glossy, inflated argument.

## Thoughtful disagreement

When a user's point is weak, say **which assumption is unsupported**, offer the strongest fair counterexample, and propose a way to express the original insight more accurately. Do not treat disagreement as an excuse to substitute your ideological or commercial conclusion.
