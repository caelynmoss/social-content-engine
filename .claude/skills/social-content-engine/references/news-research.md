# Research: truth, usefulness and timing

**News is optional supporting evidence, not the purpose of every post.** The author sets the thesis.

**For sourced depth use `references/research-depth.md` and the Information Gain Card. R0/R1/R2 are research effort settings independent of the prose mode; never confuse a Research-Driven style with completed research.**

## Research trigger

- Must attempt verification when the user asks "latest/today/this week", supplies a link with factual claims, or relies on current news, prices, product features, regulations, people or figures.
- For timeless personal observations, don't interrupt with irrelevant news.
- Sources may include public search, official announcements, dated articles, research papers, and user-provided material accessible through authorized tools. No bypassing logins or private X scraping.

## Source-to-argument bridge

1. Define **event date** and **publication date** separately. An old story reported today may not be a new development.
2. Retrieve primary evidence where possible; cross-check contentious or fast-moving claims with independent reporting.
3. Label what is VERIFIABLE FACT vs the journalist's interpretation vs the author's own inference. If sources conflict, disclose the discrepancy instead of choosing the most dramatic version.
4. Ask: **Does this news strengthen, complicate, or contradict the user's thesis?** If no meaningful connection, omit it.
5. Prefer one concrete relevant fact to five tenuous headlines. A maximum of 1–2 sourced facts per ordinary post is usually enough.
6. Source citations are outside the copy unless the author wants inline links. Keep dates and links accurate; never invent social post URLs.
7. If sources unavailable, explicitly state "I couldn't verify current news" and offer a timeless draft, not a fabricated recent headline.

## Recommendation gate

When suggesting news to write about, explain (a) why it matters to THIS author's interests, (b) what distinctive position they might plausibly explore, labelled suggestion rather than their view, and (c) what is still unverified.

Never use statistical percentages, ROI forecasts, quotes, product launch states or partnerships without provenance.
